# Magenheim Cinematic Style Guide — Peace Was Only the Beginning

Status: production authority for the Magenheim sequel cinematic
Source cinematic reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h7oYcfooCKo
Primary visual references: the user-supplied stills from the Valheim ending cinematic and the approved Magenheim storyboard/keyframes from 2026-09-29.

## Purpose

This short cinematic explicitly contradicts the implication that lasting peace returned to the Tenth World. The heroes come home believing the crisis has ended; reconnection has instead awakened old forces below the world, culminating in the reveal of the Nowhere King.

The cinematic must feel adjacent to Valheim's own illustrated/low-poly cinematic language rather than like a generic high-detail fantasy trailer.

## Core visual rule: detail belongs where detail belongs

Negative space is allowed to remain simple.

Large areas of sky, fog, distant terrain, broad stone, broad timber, roofs, floors, walls, cloth and shadow may be nothing more than a restrained flat or gently varying material. A gray-blue sky can simply be a gray-blue field. A distant mountain can be a low-detail silhouette. A wall can be a broad quiet stone plane.

Do not respond to "high detail" by encrusting every surface with seams, cracks, rivets, fractures, carvings, trim or texture noise.

Concentrate detail at:
- faces, eyes and readable silhouettes;
- hero equipment and meaningful gestures;
- the Dark Throne;
- the Nowhere King's crown, eyes, mantle edge and paired Last Argument;
- braziers, banners and runes when the camera is actually looking at them;
- structural joints where construction logic benefits the read.

The eye must always know where the important information is.

## Material language

Prefer broad, painted-looking material fields with restrained roughness variation. Do not make every surface photorealistic. Geometry and value grouping matter more than microtexture.

Surface village:
- blue-gray sky;
- subdued timber, sod/stone, dark roof surfaces and muted pines;
- warm windows/fire only where they help the eye find home and safety;
- mountains simplified by distance.

Dark Throne:
- broad basalt/blackstone fields;
- old black metal and sparse damaged royal metal;
- controlled warm brazier light against cold/neutral darkness;
- violet/red supernatural accents reserved for the King and his weapons.

## Movement language

No arbitrary image warping, moving rectangular selections, or wobbling patches.

Motion must come from objects behaving like objects:
- characters walk, stop, breathe, shift weight, turn, look, reach and draw;
- cloaks and banners follow primary motion with delayed secondary motion;
- smoke rises from a source and drifts with coherent air movement;
- fire belongs to a fire source;
- ravens flap, bank, perch or take off;
- camera movement uses deliberate dolly/pan/crane motion with easing and real parallax;
- the rumble affects the scene as a whole and causes delayed character/environment reactions;
- the Nowhere King stands from the throne-side position on an intentional trajectory;
- Firmament and Null Gate are physically drawn by his arms/hands and remain attached to weapon geometry.

Most of the frame does not need to move.

## Asset-lock policy

Storyboard objects must resolve to one of:
1. an existing Magenheim/Valheim world asset;
2. a composition of ordinary world/building pieces;
3. a deliberately authored new asset that can later exist in-world.

Do not add decorative props only because they make a concept frame look more "fantasy."

Currently locked authoritative Magenheim sources:
- `assets/models/source/dark-throne.blend`
- `assets/models/source/nowhere-king-sword-firmament.blend`
- `assets/models/source/nowhere-king-sword-null-gate.blend`

The alpine village set is built from simple timber/stone/roof forms specifically so it corresponds to plausible world construction rather than a one-off painted backdrop.

## Eight-shot picture plan

1. **Return** — heroes walk toward the small alpine/Nordic village. Camera tracks behind/alongside.
2. **Homecoming** — restrained village reaction. Great hall and normal settlement architecture are the focus, not invented set dressing.
3. **Peace** — broad quiet view over the village; negative space and calm dominate.
4. **Rumble** — the world reacts. Heroes stop and look. Objects respond coherently to the distant disturbance.
5. **Omen** — raven / atmospheric bridge into the threat below.
6. **Throne reveal** — deliberate push into the Dark Throne hall using the real Magenheim throne source.
7. **King awakens** — shadow resolves into the Nowhere King at throne-side; eyes/crown/silhouette take priority.
8. **Last Argument** — Firmament and Null Gate emerge from beneath the mantle as actual weapon motion, ending on the complete royal silhouette.

## Current narration draft

"Our heroes returned to a world in which they believed peace would reign supreme.

But with the Tenth World reconnected, old forces stirred in the depths.

From the shadows, a new threat emerged.

The Nowhere King."

This is timing text only. It must remain separate from picture. No subtitle or narration text is to be baked into cinematic renders unless explicitly approved at final mastering.

## Production order

1. Author and verify the real 3D sets.
2. Rig/block character and prop animation.
3. Render a clean silent animation and lock picture.
4. Compose/cut music to picture.
5. Record/build final voice-over.
6. Mix.
7. Optional subtitle track remains separate unless a final burned-in version is explicitly requested.
